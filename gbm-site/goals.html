Our goals

Four goals guide our work: for the coast, for women, for policy and for the wider region.

Ecology

Living green belts

Plant about 30,000 mangrove and palmyra trees along the shoreline of Guliakhali over three years, to fight soil erosion, loss of biodiversity and rising sea levels, and then extend these belts to other vulnerable coasts.

People

Women’s climate leadership

Train coastal women to run small home nurseries and become eco-friendly entrepreneurs, creating sustainable incomes and putting women at the centre of decisions about their environment.

Knowledge

Evidence for policy

Collect grounded data, such as sapling survival rates, changes in income and local stories, that can inform local, national and regional climate adaptation plans.

Awareness

Grow the movement across Asia

Visit schools and colleges across Bangladesh to share why mangrove forests matter, and inspire women in other Asian countries to start their own women-led green initiatives.

Protecting the forest is paid work

We link conservation directly to women’s income, so the trees are cared for long after planting.

Paid caretakers

Coastal women are paid and equipped as caretakers. They check how many saplings survive and protect young trees from grazing animals and illegal cutting.

Products from the forest

Women learn to process, package and brand forest products such as Keora (mangrove apple) pickle, palm-based foods and traditional eco-friendly handicrafts.

How we reach them

Every planting follows the same four steps, from seed to grown tree.

  1. Raise saplings

    Trained women grow mangrove and palmyra saplings in community and home nurseries.

  2. Plant together

    Students, volunteers and local families plant the saplings along the coast.

  3. Care for the trees

    Families living beside the plantation look after the young trees as they grow.

  4. Measure and share

    We track how many trees survive and share what we learn with others.
